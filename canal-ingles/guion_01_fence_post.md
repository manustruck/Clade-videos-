# Script 01 — YOUR FENCE POST WILL ROT

Format: vertical short, 1080×1920, about 40 seconds, English voice-over, captions of 2–4 words.
Mascot: Norm the garden gnome (see `PROYECTO_CANAL.md`).

## Facts and sources
- Wood posts rot at the ground line, where moisture and air meet; concrete can hold water against the wood there.
- Encasing the whole post, including under it, traps water like a cup.
- Common practice: 4–6 inches of gravel under the post for drainage, concrete poured around the post (not beneath
  it), and the top of the concrete sloped away from the post, finishing just above the soil.
- Hole depth depends on fence height and local frost depth: shown on screen as “check your local frost depth”,
  not stated as a number.
- Sources: Fence Armor (“Why Do Fence Posts Rot at Ground Level?” and “Protecting a Wood Post Set In Concrete”),
  Mr. Handyman (“How to protect wood posts in ground”), Fine Homebuilding forum (“Setting fence posts”).

## Voice-over (109 words)
Your fence post will rot. And you're the one who did it.

You dug a hole, dropped in the post, and filled it with concrete. All the way around. Even underneath.

Looks solid. But you just built a cup.

Rain runs down the post and has nowhere to go. It sits right here, at the ground line. Wet wood, plus air. That's where rot starts.

A few years later… one push.

Do this instead. Six inches of gravel first, so water drains out the bottom. Pour the concrete around the post, never under it. Then slope the top, so rain runs away.

Same post. Same concrete. Twice the life.
